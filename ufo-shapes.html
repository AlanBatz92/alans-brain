ℹ️ How this census is built

Each source (book, report, interview transcript) is ingested locally and normalized into located text segments. A controlled shape vocabulary — disc, sphere, triangle, cigar, Tic-Tac, egg, cone, diamond, cube, and more — is matched against every segment, and each hit is logged with its source, its location in the text, and the surrounding snippet so the mention can be checked against the original.

This is a high-recall lexical pass. It deliberately catches everything and will include false positives (a "disc" that means a record, a "triangle" that means a love triangle). An optional AI disambiguation pass then reads each snippet and confirms whether it really describes a craft's shape (dropping the false positives) and pulls its descriptive modifiers; passages it has confirmed are marked ✓ AI-checked in the drill-down. Counts shown here are mentions, not confirmed distinct sightings — a single famous case described across ten books counts ten times. Read the snippets, not just the numbers.

Reliability tiers. Every source carries a tier marking how close it sits to the original evidence — the same discipline used across the research project:

  • Tier 1 — primary firsthand testimony or an official/government document.
  • Tier 2 — verified third-party account or corroborating public record (named investigators with a track record, on-the-record officials).
  • Tier 3 — secondary reporting or synthesis (most journalistic UFO books).
  • Tier 4 — unverified community source (forums, anonymous claims). Leads only.

A higher mention count from a Tier 3 book is not "more true" than a handful from a Tier 2 source — the tier is context for weighing what you read.
