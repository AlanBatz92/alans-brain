Cheyenne Mountain, 15 February 2022: what could it have been?

Five soldiers at the top of a flight of stairs on Fort Carson saw a still, white, panel-textured ellipsoid over Cheyenne Mountain in late morning, glanced at each other, and it was gone. Pick a candidate on the right. Each one is placed exactly where it would have to be to look the way the witnesses said: same direction, same height above the ridge, same size against a thumb at arm's length. Then play the event and see what each would have looked like, how it would have moved, and what would still be in the sky when they looked back.

How far to trust this

  • Arithmetic and geometry: high confidence. Computed in code from stated inputs and cross-checked; the page's geometry is tested against the Python model.
  • Candidate sizes and looks: moderate to illustrative. Aerostat lengths come from fact sheets; balloon, blimp, helicopter and drone sizes are from memory and marked. Shapes, textures and cloud renderings are illustrations, not measurements.
  • Looked-up records: moderate. Terrain, METARs and the sounding are archived records relayed through a fetch tool and spot-checked.
  • Witness inputs: cannot be verified. Size, height and time statements drifted across retellings between 2022 and 2026; the sliders show that spread instead of picking a winner.
  • The view itself cannot tell distance. Every candidate here produces the same angle in the same place. What separates them is motion, sound, edges, the sky record and what was there after the glance.
  • Not peer reviewed. A structured way to test an account, not a finding.
